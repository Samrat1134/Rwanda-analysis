Case Study Brief: Climate Trends & 
Modern Family Planning Access in 
Rwanda 
Topic Area: Climate Resilience x Sexual & Reproductive Health and Rights (SRHR) 
Data Sources: UNICEF Global SDG 3.7.1 Indicator Data & World Bank Climate Change 
Indicators 
1. Overview & Objective 
This desk research visualizer examines the relationship between environmental land-use factors 
and access to modern family planning services in Rwanda between 2010 and 2024. The 
objective is to evaluate whether national reproductive healthcare delivery remained consistent 
alongside agricultural land-use pressures. 
2 The Problem - 
● Heavy Reliance on Farming: Around 80% of Rwanda's land is used for farming. When 
floods, droughts, or bad weather happen, rural families face immediate financial and 
food problems. 
● Trouble Reaching Clinics: Bad weather and damaged roads make it harder for 
people—especially young women—to travel to health clinics for basic healthcare. 
● The Main Question: Can a country keep expanding family planning access even while 
dealing with climate and land challenges? 
Goal of This Project To see how family planning access changed in Rwanda between 
2010 and 2024 alongside agricultural land use, using reliable public data. 
3. Key Visual Findings 
Steady Rise in Healthcare Coverage: The percentage of women (aged 15–49) whose 
demand for family planning is satisfied with modern methods grew consistently from ~60% to 
over 72% across survey cycles. 
● Sustained Agricultural Pressure: World Bank indicators show agricultural land use 
remained steady at ~79.7% of total land area, reflecting continuous resource 
dependency. 
● Service Resilience: Despite high land utilization and environmental fluctuations, family 
planning service delivery showed strong continuity, indicating effective decentralized 
public health outreach. 
4. Practical Solutions 
● Local Health Workers: Support local village health workers to deliver family planning 
supplies directly in communities, so people don't have to travel far during heavy rains. 
● Stock Supplies Early: Store extra medicines and family planning supplies at local 
health centers before rainy seasons to prevent shortages. 
● Simple Phone Services: Use basic SMS text messages to share health advice and 
clinic hours with youth who cannot visit in person.
